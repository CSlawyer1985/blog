加州律师AI新规：可以借助工具，不能交出判断

一份 AI 起草的文书，律师已经通读，案例名称也像真的，这就可以提交了吗？加州刚刚签署的 SB 574 给出了更具体的要求：负责提交的律师须亲自核验引用；使用生成式 AI 的，还涉及保密、纠错和向法院披露等义务。

2026 年 9 月 30 日，加州州长签署 SB 574，编为 2026 年第 858 章。依加州宪法普通生效规则，该法于 2027 年 1 月 1 日生效；这一日期是结合生效规则得出的，并非 SB 574 正文直接写明。签署情况可见州长官网公告，生效依据见加州宪法第四条第 8 节。

我理解，这部法最值得中国法律人研究的，是它把使用 AI 的责任落到了具体工作环节：什么资料可以输入，什么内容必须由人核查，什么判断必须由人作出。讨论这些问题，比简单争论律师应不应该使用 AI 更有实际价值。

本文依据已签署版本公开文本分析。加州立法信息网原文链接本次访问受限，条文采用两份相互核对的公开镜像，文末保留来源说明与完整双语附件。

一、禁止交出执业判断，不等于禁止使用 AI

新设《商业与职业法典》（BPC）第 6068.1 条同时写了两件事：律师不得把法律执业委托给生成式 AI；律师使用生成式 AI 辅助执业时，须履行相应义务。法律因此保留了辅助使用的空间，也明确保留了律师原有的合理胜任与勤勉义务。依据：SB 574 第 1 节。

实践中的分界，不宜只看文书由谁敲字。AI 可以参与整理、起草，但法律问题怎样识别、证据能否支撑主张、论证是否适用，仍需要律师作出实质判断。法条没有列出每种功能的许可清单，上述是从辅助使用与不得委托执业并存所作的解释，不能把“最后有人点了确认”直接视为合规。

这对高度自动化的工作流尤其有提醒价值。审核节点应当放在判断发生的地方。等全部流程跑完，再让律师看一眼成稿，很难说明此前的实质判断究竟由谁完成。

二、保密条件看谁能访问，不能只看“企业版”三个字

第 6068.1(a)(3)(A) 条的结构容易被读错。它并非一概禁止向 AI 输入保密、个人身份识别及其他非公开信息，而是禁止向不满足特定访问限制的系统输入这些信息。访问者须限于律师本人，以及经律师授权、负有保护信息保密义务的人。依据：SB 574 第 1 节。

所以，“付费”“企业版”“不用于训练”都不能单独替代法定条件。不用于训练回答的是一种数据用途，访问控制回答的是谁能接触数据，两者并不相同。采购和使用前，应核查合同与实际配置，尤其是服务商人员访问、分包商处理、共享链接及插件接入。法条未逐项规定这些技术细节，这是落实访问与保密条件时的审查建议。

信息范围也不只是客户姓名。法条列举了出生日期、当事人与证人的地址电话、医疗或精神健康信息、财务信息、账号，以及法院封存或依法保密的其他内容。仅替换公司名称后把整份材料送入系统，未必已经解决问题。

三、合理核验 AI 输出，与亲自核验法院文件引用，是两项义务

第 6068.1 条要求律师采取合理措施，核验生成式 AI 输出的准确性，包括全部案例与法条引用；对律师使用的材料，还须纠正错误或幻觉内容。它关注的是律师怎样使用 AI 输出。

经修订的《民事诉讼法典》（CCP）第 128.7(b)(2)(A) 条另规定：向法院提交的诉辩书、动议等文件，不得包含负责提交的律师未亲自核验的任何引用，其中包括 AI 提供的引用。这里的“任何引用”没有限于 AI 来源。让 AI 再确认一遍，或者仅接受同事“已查过”的结论，不能直接替代负责提交律师本人的核验。依据：SB 574 第 1、3 节。

但也不应把条文加码成“团队每位签字律师都必须重复完成全部核验”。原文落点是负责提交的律师，没有在这里规定团队内部所有人的重复审核方式。第 128.7(g) 条还排除了证据开示中的披露，以及证据开示请求、答复、异议和动议，不能把第 128.7 条写成毫无例外地覆盖所有文件；独立的第 6068.1 条义务仍须分别判断。

对律所管理而言，最有用的改进是一张引用核验表：原始出处在哪里，具体命题能否得到支持，现行版本是否适用，谁核验、何时核验。案例确实存在，只完成了核验的一部分。它是否真的支持文书里的那句话，同样需要人读原文。这是流程建议，并非法定表格。

四、向法院披露是义务，面向公众则是考虑是否披露

第 6068.1(a)(3)(C) 条要求律师就向法院提交的所有文件披露生成式 AI 的使用；用 AI 创作面向公众的内容时，则须考虑是否披露。两个场景的措辞不同，不能都译成强制披露，也不能都写成律师自行决定。依据：SB 574 第 1 节。

该法没有给出固定披露格式、最低使用量或普通编辑工具的豁免清单，也没有直接回答每一种混合功能软件的边界。办理具体案件时，还需要核查适用的法院规则和法官命令。律所可以先记录 AI 参与了哪个环节，再由负责律师判断具体披露方式；自行拟定的模板不应宣传成法定安全港。

披露解决的是透明度。已经披露使用 AI，仍须履行核验和纠错义务。

五、仲裁员不能用披露换取委托 AI 决策

新设 CCP 第 1282.1 条对仲裁员规定了两道要求：不得将决策过程的任何部分委托给生成式 AI；依赖 AI 生成的案卷外信息前，须向当事人作适当披露，并在切实可行的范围内允许其评论该信息的使用。依据：SB 574 第 5 节。

后一道要求并没有消除前一道禁令。即使披露了，也不能因此把判断证人可信度、选择裁判依据或决定责任比例交给 AI。反过来，条文写的是事先披露及评论机会，不是要求双方同意一切 AI 使用。把它概括成“仲裁员完全不得使用 AI”或“双方同意即可让 AI 裁决”，都超出了文本。

这部法还处理了容易被简讯遗漏的制度问题。第 2 节修订的是 BPC 第 6173 条，并非“6173.7”：它完善 ADR 自愿认证及投诉的受理、调查、处理程序，设置资料提供、特权保护和保密限制。其中第 6173(b)(2)(B) 项规定的资料提供机制，只适用于一方自行代理的调解，并保留相关特权材料须经各权利人书面同意等限制。第 4 节则新增 CCP 第 180 条，要求司法委员会公开重新审视 Standard 10.80，并按需要修订。附件保留这些内容，不能只附三条 AI 义务。

六、可能承担制裁，但不是发现错误就自动罚款

第 128.7 条把责任连接到既有程序：通知、合理答辩机会，以及法院对是否违反该条的判断。对方申请制裁，要另行动议，先送达，原则上待 21 天或法院另定期间内仍未撤回、适当纠正后，才能提交法院；法院自行启动的说明理由令，条文也写明送达后 21 天的撤回或适当纠正安排。依据：SB 574 第 3 节第 128.7(c) 款。

这些安排不应被宣传成“先提交，出事再改”的许可。制裁的形式和范围受法条限制；第 (d)(1) 项对有律师代理的当事人因第 (b)(2) 项违规，排除了金钱制裁，不能据此概括为所有当事人都不承担任何后果。该条也不能替代对其他责任依据的分析。

律所责任尤其不能翻反。原文是“Absent exceptional circumstances”，意思是除特殊情形外，律所应对其合伙人、受雇律师和员工的违规承担共同责任。“jointly responsible”在这里译为共同责任，不再扩张成原文没有写出的“jointly and severally”。这要求管理者关注工具准入、培训和复核安排，不能只给个人发一份免责声明。

七、中国法律人可以借鉴流程，不能直接搬用法域结论

SB 574 是加州立法。不能因为中国律师使用了同一款 AI，便说其普遍受该法约束；涉及加州执业资格、当地程序或跨境项目时，应另行识别适用规则。中国律师自身的执业、保密与数据处理义务，仍须按中国法和具体业务场景判断。

我更愿意把这次立法理解成一份流程改造提示。文件进入系统前，查明资料属性和可访问者；AI 生成之后，把事实、引文和法律判断分开核查；提交之前，明确负责律师及适用披露要求；发现错误时，保留纠正记录并及时处理程序后果。这些工作都可以分配，但负责人的判断不能在分配中消失。

AI 能把起草时间压缩得很短。律师需要为核验留出真实的时间，并留下能回到原始依据的记录。

附件一：SB 574 最终签署版本英文全文

Senate Bill No. 574

CHAPTER 858

An act to amend Section 6173 of, and to add Section 6068.1 to, the Business and Professions Code, and to amend Section 128.7 of, and to add Sections 180 and 1282.1 to, the Code of Civil Procedure, relating to civil proceedings.

[ Approved by Governor  September 30, 2026. Filed with Secretary of State  September 30, 2026. ]

LEGISLATIVE COUNSEL'S DIGEST

SB 574, Umberg. Attorneys, arbitrators, judicial officers, and alternative resolution providers.

Existing law, the State Bar Act, provides for the licensure and regulation of attorneys by the State Bar of California, a public corporation. The act requires an attorney to strictly maintain client confidences and to preserve client secrets at their own peril.

This bill would prohibit an attorney from delegating the practice of law to generative artificial intelligence. The bill would require an attorney who uses generative artificial intelligence to assist in the practice of law to, among other things, not enter confidential, personal identifying, and other nonpublic information into a generative artificial intelligence system, as specified.

The bill would also require an attorney to take reasonable steps to verify the accuracy of generative artificial intelligence outputs and to correct any erroneous or hallucinated output in any material used by the attorney.

Existing law requires every pleading, petition, written notice of motion, or other similar paper to be signed by the attorney of record, or if a party is unrepresented, by the party, thereby certifying to the best of the person’s knowledge, information, and belief that it is not being presented primarily for an improper purpose and that the claims, defenses, and legal and factual contentions are warranted, as specified.

This bill would prohibit a brief, pleading, motion, or any other paper filed in any court from containing any citations that an attorney responsible for submitting the pleading has not personally verified, including any citation provided by generative artificial intelligence.

Existing law, the California Arbitration Act, provides a statutory framework for the enforcement of contractual arbitration under California law. The act establishes that a written agreement to submit a present or future controversy to arbitration is valid, enforceable, and irrevocable, except as specified.

The act defines a neutral arbitrator as one who is selected jointly by the parties or by the parties’ arbitrators, or is appointed by the court if the parties or their arbitrators cannot jointly select an arbitrator. The act requires a person selected to serve as a neutral arbitrator to disclose all matters that could cause a person aware of the facts to reasonably entertain a doubt as to the proposed neutral arbitrator’s impartiality.

This bill would prohibit an arbitrator from delegating any part of their decisionmaking process to any generative artificial intelligence tool, and would prohibit an arbitrator from relying on information generated by generative artificial intelligence outside the record without making appropriate disclosures to the parties beforehand, as specified.

The California Constitution establishes the Judicial Council, and requires the Judicial Council to adopt rules for court administration, practice, and procedure, and to undertake certain responsibilities with regard to court facilities.

This bill would require the Judicial Council to publicly revisit a specified standard of judicial administration to incorporate any necessary changes reflecting the further development of generative artificial intelligence.

Existing law requires the State Bar to create a program to certify alternative resolution firms, providers, or practitioners.

Existing law requires the program to include procedures to verify, among other things, that a firm, provider, or practitioner has procedures in place for persons to make complaints regarding the failure of an arbitrator or mediator of the firm, provider, or practitioner to comply with ethical standards, as applicable, and procedures to remedy failures of arbitrators or mediators to comply with those standards.

This bill would instead require the State Bar to adopt procedures for the State Bar or a professional organization to receive, investigate, and resolve any complaints that a certified firm, provider, or practitioner failed to comply with the applicable ethical standards of conduct, as specified. The bill would require complaint proceedings to occur in private and be confidential.

After a decision on a complaint, the bill would authorize the State Bar to publicly disclose information or records concerning complaint proceedings that do not reveal confidential or privileged communications. The bill would exempt information, records, or communications provided under these provisions from disclosure under the Public Records Act.

Existing constitutional provisions require that a statute that limits the right of access to the meetings of public bodies or the writings of public officials and agencies be adopted with findings demonstrating the interest protected by the limitation and the need for protecting that interest.

This bill would make legislative findings to that effect.

Vote: MAJORITY   Appropriation: NO   Fiscal Committee: YES   Local Program: NO

The people of the State of California do enact as follows:

SECTION 1.

Section 6068.1 is added to the Business and Professions Code, to read:

6068.1.

(a) (1) Nothing in this section shall be construed to abrogate an attorney’s duty to exercise reasonable competence and diligence in the practice of law.

(2) An attorney shall not delegate the practice of law to generative artificial intelligence.

(3) An attorney who uses generative artificial intelligence to assist in the practice of law shall do all of the following:

(A) Not enter confidential, personal identifying, and other nonpublic information into a generative artificial intelligence system for which access to confidential, personal identifying, or other nonpublic information the attorney inputs into the system is not restricted to the attorney and persons authorized by the attorney under obligations to protect the confidentiality of the information.

(B) Take reasonable steps to do both of the following:

(i) Verify the accuracy of generative artificial intelligence outputs, including, but not limited to, the accuracy of all case and statutory citations.

(ii) Correct any erroneous or hallucinated output in any material used by the attorney.

(C) Disclose the use of generative artificial intelligence to the court for all documents submitted to the court and consider whether to disclose the use of generative artificial intelligence if it is used to create content provided to the public.

(b) For purposes of this section:

(1) “Generative artificial intelligence” means an artificial intelligence system that can generate derived synthetic content, including text, images, video, and audio that emulates the structure and characteristics of the system’s training data.

(2) “Personal identifying information” includes all the following:

(A) Driver’s license numbers.

(B) Dates of birth.

(C) Social security numbers.

(D) National Crime Information and Criminal Identification and Information numbers.

(E) Addresses and phone numbers of parties, victims, witnesses, and court personnel.

(F) Medical or psychiatric information.

(G) Financial information.

(H) Account numbers.

(I) Any other content sealed by court order or deemed confidential by court rule or statute.

SEC. 2.

Section 6173 of the Business and Professions Code is amended to read:

6173.

(a) (1) The State Bar shall create a voluntary program to certify alternative dispute resolution firms, providers, or practitioners.

(2) A program created pursuant to paragraph (1) shall not require a firm, provider, or practitioner to be a licensee of the State Bar in order to be certified under the program.

(b) The program shall include, but shall not be limited to, all of the following:

(1) Procedures established by the State Bar for a firm, provider, or practitioner to become a certified alternative dispute resolution firm, provider, or practitioner that include, but are not limited to, a requirement that the firm, provider, or practitioner verify all of the following:

(A) The firm, provider, or practitioner requires, at a minimum, its arbitrators to comply with the Ethics Standards for Neutral Arbitrators in Contractual Arbitration as adopted by the Judicial Council pursuant to Section 1281.85 of the Code of Civil Procedure.

(B) The firm, provider, or practitioner requires, at a minimum, its mediators to comply with ethical standards that are equivalent to the Rules of Conduct for Mediators in Court-Connected Mediation Programs for General Civil Cases as provided in Rules 3.850 to 3.860, inclusive, of the California Rules of Court.

(2) Procedures for the State Bar or a professional organization approved by the State Bar to receive, investigate, and resolve any complaints that a certified firm, provider, or practitioner failed to comply with the applicable ethical standards of conduct.

(A) For mediators, those complaint procedures shall be substantially similar to the complaint procedures specified in Article 3 (commencing with Rule 3.865) of Chapter 3 of Division 8 of Title 3 of the California Rules of Court.

(B) Notwithstanding subdivision (e) of Section 6068, Chapter 2 (commencing with Section 1115) of Division 9 of the Evidence Code, the Lawyer-Client Privilege Article 3 (commencing with Section 950) of Chapter 4 of Division 8 of the Evidence Code, the Attorney Work Product doctrine as restated in Chapter 4 (commencing with Section 2018.010) of Title 4 of Part 4 of the Code of Civil Procedure, or any other law, alternative dispute resolution firms, providers, or practitioners certified by the State Bar pursuant to Section 6173, licensees of the State Bar, limited liability partnerships, or law corporations registered with the State Bar shall, pursuant to a request made as part of a procedure to address a complaint that a firm, provider, or practitioner violated applicable ethical standards, provide to the State Bar or its agents all requested information, records, or communications relevant to the conduct alleged in the complaint.

This subparagraph is subject to clauses (i), (ii), and (iii).

(i) A person is not required under this subparagraph to provide information, records, or communications protected by the lawyer-client privilege or the attorney work product doctrine, without regard to which person has possession or custody of the material, unless each holder of the privilege or protection has consented in writing to its production. A person who submits a complaint under this paragraph is deemed to have consented to the production of material for which that person is the sole holder.

(ii) Information, records, or communications provided under this subparagraph shall be used solely for the receipt, investigation, and resolution of the complaint, and shall not be disclosed to any person other than the person who submitted the complaint, the firm, provider, or practitioner that is the subject of the complaint, a holder of a privilege or protection in the material, and State Bar personnel and agents assigned to the proceeding.

(iii) This subparagraph shall only apply to a mediation where one of the parties is self-represented.

(C) By providing any information, records, or communications under subparagraph (B), a person described in subparagraph (B) does not violate, waive, or extinguish, and a holder of a privilege or protection in the material does not waive or extinguish, any privilege, protection, or duty of confidentiality under subdivision (e) of Section 6068, Chapter 2 (commencing with Section 1115) of Division 9 of the Evidence Code, the Lawyer-Client Privilege under Article 3 (commencing with Section 950) of Chapter 4 of Division 8 of the Evidence Code, the Attorney Work Product doctrine as restated in Chapter 4 (commencing with Section 2018.010) of Title 4 of Part 4 of the Code of Civil Procedure, or the protections of any other rule or law.

(D) All complaint proceedings shall occur in private and be kept confidential. No confidential or privileged information, records, or communications concerning the receipt, investigation, or resolution of a complaint may be disclosed outside the course of the complaint proceeding, except as provided in subparagraph (E) or as required by an order of a court of competent jurisdiction.

(E) After a decision on a complaint, the State Bar may publicly disclose information or records concerning the complaint proceeding that do not reveal any confidential or privileged communications, including the name of a certified firm, provider, or practitioner that has been found to have violated an applicable ethical standard, any action that has been taken to remedy the violation, and the general basis on which the action was taken.

(F) Any information, records, or communications provided under this paragraph shall not be disclosed pursuant to any state law, including, but not limited to, the California Public Records Act (Division 10 (commencing with Section 7920.000) of Title 1 of the Government Code).

(3) Different levels or tiers for certification that meet both of the following requirements:

(A) Higher levels or tiers are awarded to firms, providers, or practitioners that demonstrate a higher level of commitment to accountability and consumer protection based on criteria or other standards, including, but not limited to, additional education or training, adopted by the State Bar.

(B) The levels or tiers do not reflect an assessment of the quality of a firm, provider, or practitioner.

(4) Authority for the State Bar to deny or revoke certification for a failure to meet or maintain certification standards.

(5) The professional organization approved by the State Bar pursuant to paragraph (2) shall not be a voluntary association of attorneys.

(c) (1) The State Bar may charge an alternative dispute resolution firm, provider, or practitioner seeking certification pursuant to this section a fee to cover the reasonable costs of administering the program.

(2) The fee charged pursuant to paragraph (1) may be higher in the early years of the program to reimburse the State Bar for costs related to the planning and establishment of the program, including, but not limited to, technology and preliminary staffing costs.

(3) The State Bar shall not use any moneys received from the annual license fees collected pursuant to Section 6140 or 6141 for any costs associated with this section.

(d) For purposes of this article:

(1) “Alternative dispute resolution” means mediation, arbitration, conciliation, or other nonjudicial procedure that involves a neutral party in the decisionmaking process.

(2) “Program” means the program created pursuant to subdivision (a).

128.7.

(a) Every pleading, petition, written notice of motion, or other similar paper shall be signed by at least one attorney of record in the attorney’s individual name, or, if the party is not represented by an attorney, shall be signed by the party. (A) It is not being presented primarily for an improper purpose, such as to harass or to cause unnecessary delay or needless increase in the cost of litigation.

(B) The claims, defenses, and other legal contentions therein are warranted by existing law or by a nonfrivolous argument for the extension, modification, or reversal of existing law or the establishment of new law.

In determining what sanctions, if any, should be ordered, the court shall consider whether a party seeking sanctions has exercised due diligence.

(1) A motion for sanctions under this section shall be made separately from other motions or requests and shall describe the specific conduct alleged to violate subdivision (b). Notice of motion shall be served as provided in Section 1010, but shall not be filed with or presented to the court unless, within 21 days after service of the motion, or any other period as the court may prescribe, the challenged paper, claim, defense, contention, allegation, or denial is not withdrawn or appropriately corrected.

(d) A sanction imposed for violation of subdivision (b) shall be limited to what is sufficient to deter repetition of this conduct or comparable conduct by others similarly situated.

Subject to the limitations in paragraphs (1) and (2), the sanction may consist of, or include, directives of a nonmonetary nature, an order to pay a penalty into court, or, if imposed on motion and warranted for effective deterrence, an order directing payment to the movant of some or all of the reasonable attorney’s fees and other expenses incurred as a direct result of the violation.

(1) Monetary sanctions may not be awarded against a represented party for a violation of paragraph (2) of subdivision (b).

(2) Monetary sanctions may not be awarded on the court’s motion unless the court issues its order to show cause before a voluntary dismissal or settlement of the claims made by or against the party that is, or whose attorneys are, to be sanctioned.

(e) When imposing sanctions, the court shall describe the conduct determined to constitute a violation of this section and explain the basis for the sanction imposed.

(f) In addition to any award pursuant to this section for conduct described in subdivision (b), the court may assess punitive damages against the plaintiff upon a determination by the court that the plaintiff’s action was an action maintained by a person convicted of a felony against the person’s victim, or the victim’s heirs, relatives, estate, or personal representative, for injuries arising from the acts for which the person was convicted of a felony, and that the plaintiff is guilty of fraud, oppression, or malice in maintaining the action.

(g) This section shall not apply to disclosures and discovery requests, responses, objections, and motions.

(h) A motion for sanctions brought by a party or a party’s attorney primarily for an improper purpose, such as to harass or to cause unnecessary delay or needless increase in the cost of litigation, shall itself be subject to a motion for sanctions. It is the intent of the Legislature that courts shall vigorously use its sanctions authority to deter that improper conduct or comparable conduct by others similarly situated.

(i) This section shall apply to a complaint or petition filed on or after January 1, 1995, and any other pleading, written notice of motion, or other similar paper filed in that matter.

一项关于民事程序的法律，修订《商业与职业法典》第 6173 条并增设第 6068.1 条，修订《民事诉讼法典》第 128.7 条并增设第 180 条和第 1282.1 条。

立法顾问摘要

SB 574，Umberg。律师、仲裁员、司法人员及替代性争议解决服务提供者。

现行《州律师协会法》规定，由加州州律师协会这一公法人负责律师的执业许可与监管。该法要求律师严格维护客户的保密事项，并自行承担保护客户秘密的责任。

本法案将禁止律师把法律执业委托给生成式人工智能。对于使用生成式人工智能辅助法律执业的律师，本法案将要求其履行若干义务，其中包括依照具体规定，不得向生成式人工智能系统输入保密信息、个人身份识别信息及其他非公开信息。

本法案还将要求律师采取合理措施，核验生成式人工智能输出的准确性，并纠正律师使用的任何材料中的错误或幻觉输出。

现行法要求每份诉辩文书、申请书、书面动议通知或其他类似文件由在案代理律师签署；当事人没有律师代理的，由当事人签署。这种签署证明，依签署人所知、所得信息及所持信念，该文件并非主要为不正当目的而提出，且其中的请求、抗辩及法律与事实主张具有相应依据，具体依有关规定。

本法案将禁止向任何法院提交的书面论证、诉辩文书、动议或其他文件包含负责提交诉辩文书的律师未亲自核验的任何引用，包括由生成式人工智能提供的引用。

现行《加州仲裁法》为执行加州法下的合同仲裁提供了法定框架。除具体规定的情形外，该法确认，将现有或未来争议提交仲裁的书面协议有效、可执行且不可撤销。

该法将中立仲裁员定义为由当事人共同选定、由各方选定的仲裁员共同选定，或者在当事人或其选定的仲裁员不能共同选定时由法院指定的仲裁员。该法要求获选担任中立仲裁员的人披露所有可能使了解事实的人合理怀疑其公正性的事项。

本法案将禁止仲裁员把决策过程的任何部分委托给任何生成式人工智能工具，并将依具体规定，禁止仲裁员在未事先向当事人作适当披露的情况下依赖生成式人工智能生成的案卷外信息。

《加州宪法》设立司法委员会，要求其制定法院行政、实务及程序规则，并就法院设施承担若干职责。

本法案将要求司法委员会公开重新审视一项指定的司法行政标准，纳入为反映生成式人工智能进一步发展而作出的任何必要修改。

现行法要求州律师协会设立一项对替代性争议解决机构、服务提供者或执业者进行认证的计划。

现行法要求该计划包含核查程序，其中包括核查机构、服务提供者或执业者是否建立程序，供人们就其仲裁员或调解员未遵守适用伦理标准的情形提出投诉，以及是否建立纠正仲裁员或调解员未遵守这些标准的程序。

本法案将改为要求州律师协会制定程序，由州律师协会或专业组织受理、调查并处理关于获认证的机构、服务提供者或执业者未遵守适用伦理行为标准的投诉，具体依有关规定。投诉程序应当不公开进行，并予以保密。

投诉作出决定后，本法案将授权州律师协会公开披露不揭露任何保密或受特权保护通信的投诉程序信息或记录。本法案将使依据这些规定提供的信息、记录或通信免于依《公共记录法》披露。

现行宪法规定，法律若限制公众查阅公共机构会议或公职人员及机关文件的权利，须作出立法认定，说明该限制所保护的利益及保护该利益的必要性。

本法案将就此作出相应立法认定。

《民事诉讼法典》第 128.7 条修订如下：

128.7.

(a) 每份诉辩文书、申请书、书面动议通知或其他类似文件，应当由至少一名在案代理律师以其个人名义签署；当事人没有律师代理的，应由当事人签署。每份文件应当注明签署人的地址及电话号码（如有）。除法律另有规定外，诉辩文书无须另作真实性确认，也无须附宣誓书。

未签署的文件应予剔除，但律师或当事人在被告知漏签后及时补正签名的除外。

(b)(1) 律师或自行代理的当事人通过签署、提交备案、递交或其后继续主张等方式，向法院呈交诉辩文书、申请书、书面动议通知或其他类似文件，即证明其经过与具体情形相适应的合理调查后，依其所知、所得信息及所持信念，下列全部条件均已满足：

(A) 文件并非主要为不正当目的而提出，例如骚扰、造成不必要的拖延，或无谓增加诉讼费用。

(B) 其中的请求、抗辩及其他法律主张，具有现行法依据，或具有并非无理的、支持扩展、修改、推翻现行法律或创设新法的论证依据。

(C) 所述事实及其他事实主张有证据支持；或者，如已特别作出说明，在获得合理的进一步调查或证据开示机会后，可能获得证据支持。

(D) 对事实主张的否认具有证据依据；或者，如已特别作出说明，是合理基于缺乏信息或信念而作出的。

(2)(A) 向任何法院提交的书面论证、诉辩文书、动议或其他任何文件，不得包含负责提交诉辩文书的律师未亲自核验的任何引用，包括由生成式人工智能提供的任何引用。

(B) 在本项中，“生成式人工智能”是指能够生成衍生合成内容的人工智能系统，包括模拟其训练数据结构与特征的文本、图像、视频及音频。

(c) 经通知并给予合理答辩机会后，法院如认定违反了第 (b) 款，可以在下列条件限制下，对违反该款或应对违规负责的律师、律师事务所或当事人施加适当制裁。法院决定是否施加制裁及施加何种制裁时，应考虑申请制裁的当事人是否已尽勤勉义务。

(1) 依据本条提出的制裁动议，应当独立于其他动议或请求提出，并应描述被指违反第 (b) 款的具体行为。动议通知应依第 1010 条送达；但不得向法院提交备案或呈交，除非在动议送达后 21 天内，或法院规定的其他期间内，被质疑的文件、请求、抗辩、主张、事实陈述或否认未被撤回或适当纠正。

如有正当理由，法院可以向该动议的胜诉方判给其为提出或反对动议而发生的合理费用与律师费。除特殊情形外，律师事务所应对其合伙人、受雇律师及员工实施的违规承担共同责任。

(2) 法院可以依职权作出命令，描述看来违反第 (b) 款的具体行为，并责令律师、律师事务所或当事人说明为何其没有违反该款；但在该说明理由令送达后 21 天内，被质疑的文件、请求、抗辩、主张、事实陈述或否认已被撤回或适当纠正的除外。

(d) 因违反第 (b) 款施加的制裁，应以足以遏制重复实施该行为，或遏制处境相似的其他人实施类似行为的程度为限。

在第 (1) 项及第 (2) 项限制下，制裁可以是或包括非金钱性质的指令、向法院缴纳罚款的命令；或者，在依动议施加制裁且为有效遏制违规而有必要时，责令向动议申请人支付因违规直接产生的部分或全部合理律师费及其他费用。

(1) 对于第 (b) 款第 (2) 项的违规，不得向有律师代理的当事人施加金钱制裁。

(2) 法院依职权施加金钱制裁的，必须在拟受制裁当事人所提或针对该当事人的请求，或者拟受制裁律师所代理当事人所涉请求，自愿撤诉或和解之前，发出说明理由令；否则不得施加金钱制裁。

(e) 施加制裁时，法院应当描述被认定违反本条的行为，并说明所施制裁的依据。

(f) 对第 (b) 款所述行为，除依据本条判给的任何款项外，如法院认定原告曾因重罪被定罪，却就其所实施重罪行为造成的损害，向受害人或受害人的继承人、亲属、遗产或个人代表提起并维持诉讼，且原告维持该诉讼具有欺诈、压迫或恶意，法院还可以对原告判处惩罚性赔偿。

(g) 本条不适用于证据开示中的披露，以及证据开示请求、答复、异议和动议。

(h) 当事人或其律师主要为不正当目的，例如骚扰、造成不必要的拖延或无谓增加诉讼费用，而提出制裁动议的，该动议本身亦可成为制裁动议的对象。立法机关意在要求法院积极行使制裁权，以遏制这些不当行为或处境相似的其他人实施的类似行为。

(i) 本条适用于 1995 年 1 月 1 日或之后提交的起诉状或申请书，以及该案件中提交的任何其他诉辩文书、书面动议通知或其他类似文件。

第 4 节

在《民事诉讼法典》中增设第 180 条，内容如下：

  1. 司法委员会应当公开重新审视《加州司法行政标准》第 10.80 号标准，并在必要时修订，纳入为反映生成式人工智能进一步发展而需作出的任何必要修改。

第 5 节

在《民事诉讼法典》中增设第 1282.1 条，内容如下：

1282.1.

(a) 本条的任何规定不得解释为取消仲裁员的中立义务以及履行合理胜任与勤勉的义务。

(b)(1) 仲裁员不得把其决策过程的任何部分委托给任何生成式人工智能工具。

(2) 仲裁员不得在未事先向当事人作出适当披露，并在切实可行的范围内允许当事人评论其使用的情况下，依赖生成式人工智能生成的案卷外信息。

(c) 在本条中，“生成式人工智能”是指能够生成衍生合成内容的人工智能系统，包括模拟其训练数据结构与特征的文本、图像、视频及音频。

第 6 节

立法机关认定并声明，本法第 2 节对《商业与职业法典》第 6173 条的修订，构成《加州宪法》第一条第 3 节所指的，对公众查阅公共机构会议或公职人员及机关文件权利的限制。

依据该宪法规定，立法机关作出以下认定，以说明该限制所保护的利益及保护该利益的必要性：

为保护州律师协会或其依据《商业与职业法典》第 6173 条认可的专业组织在调查中所披露的受特权保护记录及通信的保密性，有必要使这些记录及通信免于披露。
